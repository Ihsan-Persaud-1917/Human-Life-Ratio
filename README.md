## <span style="color:#C5C7C9">✳</span> The Vision

A price tells you how much money something costs. It doesn't tell you how much of your life you must spend earning it.

**Human Life Ratio (HLR)** explores a different way to compare affordability across countries: the working hours required for a country's median earner to afford everyday goods and services.

Because economic statistics describe economies. **HLR aims to bring the human experience back into the equation.**

## <span style="color:#A64C4C">◈</span> The Idea

1. Calculate how many working hours an item costs in each country.
2. Compare those hours against the country with the lowest requirement for that item.
3. Average the resulting ratios across a consistent basket of goods and services.

<p align="center">
  <strong>Item Ratio</strong> = Working Hours Required ÷ Lowest Observed Working Hours
  <br /><br />
  <strong>HLR</strong> = Sum of Item Ratios ÷ Number of Items
</p>

A ratio of **1.0×** for an individual item represents the lowest observed working-time requirement among the countries included in that comparison. Higher ratios indicate a greater working-time burden relative to that benchmark.

<sub>The overall score averages item-level ratios; it is not a literal measure of total living costs or quality of life.</sub>

## <span style="color:#748B83">◎</span> Design Philosophy

HLR should feel like a precision instrument for exploring economic reality.

| Principle | Direction |
|:--|:--|
| <span style="color:#C5C7C9">**Human-centred**</span> | Measure affordability in working time. |
| <span style="color:#C5C7C9">**Transparent**</span> | Make calculations, sources, and limitations visible. |
| <span style="color:#C5C7C9">**Precise**</span> | Consistent comparisons, clear data, deliberate structure. |
| <span style="color:#C5C7C9">**Distinctive**</span> | Obsidian black, titanium lines, and restrained oxide-red accents. |

**Visual identity:** `#101114` · `#C5C7C9` · `#A64C4C` · `#748B83`

Sharp geometry. Fine borders. No unnecessary pills.
